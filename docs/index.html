      How to play
      

        
A turn is three steps, in order. Draw one card — the top of the
        stock, or the face-up discard. Then lay your phase down if you can, and
        play spare cards onto any group already on the table. Then discard one
        card, which ends your turn.


        
Laying down needs the whole phase at once, and you only do it
        once a round. After that it stays down whatever happens next.


        
Hitting is how you empty your hand, and you can only do it
        once your own phase is down. A set takes another card of its rank, a
        run takes either end and grows as it does, a colour group takes another
        card of its colour, and no group ever takes a Skip.


        
Going out is shedding your last card, by discarding it or by
        hitting it onto a group. The round ends there for everyone.


        
Scoring is golf: low is good. Whatever is still in your hand
        when the round ends is what it costs you — 5 a card for 1 to 9, 10 for
        10 to 12, 15 for a Skip and 25 for a Wild. Going out costs nothing.
        Laying your phase down still clears it even if you are caught holding
        cards; you just pay for them.


        
A Wild stands in for any card, and you choose what it is when
        you lay down — a run of 4 from W 4 5 6 can be 3-4-5-6 or
        4-5-6-7, and that decides what the group will take afterwards. Every
        group needs at least one real card.


        
A Skip is never part of a phase. Play it to look at the top
        three of the stock and keep one, free — it costs no draw, and the Skip
        becomes that turn's discard.


        
Runs do not wrap: 11-12-1 is not a run.
